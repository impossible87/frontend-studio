# Choose a direction from the brief

Decide theme/purpose → content hierarchy → typography/layout → color/assets → motion. Choose one coherent language. A familiar typeface, centered composition, serif heading, card, or grid is appropriate when it serves the content. Single-family typography can be excellent. Avoid arbitrary font mixing and layout variation introduced merely to look different.

## Direction vocabulary

These are starting points, not complete formulas or required font/color bundles:

| Direction | Useful content/context | Structural emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Editorial | Essays, research, thoughtful brands | Reading rhythm, clear sections, strong text |
| Quiet product | Tools and services | Stable controls, direct explanation, restrained demonstration |
| Swiss/structured | Systems and technical work | Explicit grid, alignment, concise hierarchy |
| Gallery | Selected art/design work | Image relationships and generous separation |
| Architectural | Spaces, materials, professional studios | Proportion, texture, measured composition |
| Luxury restraint | Craft and considered products | Few deliberate elements, real asset quality |
| Playful | Friendly creative products | Clear interaction with purposeful surprise |
| Industrial | Manufacturing and technical stories | Practical structure and material cues |
| Expressive/brutalist | Appropriate cultural/editorial projects | Intentional tension with usable controls |
| Warm human | Community and personal services | Specific language and approachable rhythm |
| Scientific | Evidence and complex comparison | Precision, annotations, stable data roles |
| Narrative | A sequence that benefits from staging | Scene relationships with immediate access |

Describe the chosen direction using actual decisions: density, alignment, visual anchor, type roles, asset treatment, surfaces, and animation purpose. Do not substitute a direction label for those decisions.

## Diagnose disorder conditionally

Look for competing oversized headings, unrelated display fonts, repetitive decorative labels, cards inside cards, unnecessary borders, disconnected alignment, generic stock imagery, repeated fade-up sequences, and effects that obscure controls. Ask what each communicates. Retain useful patterns; simplify redundant emphasis. Build scale and space around one primary focal point, rather than making every section equally loud.

Compare two representative compositions with real copy if a choice is uncertain. Choose inside the user's established theme. Search/observation follows 17, creative conversion follows 18; do not repeat research here. Inspect a grayscale skeleton and one running representative section before extending the language.
