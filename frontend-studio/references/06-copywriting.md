# Specific copy and useful actions

Preserve real facts, product names, prices, contact details, and existing legal terms unless rewriting is authorized. Do not invent metrics, testimonials, customer logos, awards, or scientific results. Label samples and TODOs clearly.

Write a heading that says what the product, service, or section actually offers. Explain who it helps and how. Use evidence the user supplied. Calls to action name the next action or destination; vague slogans and generic "learn more" everywhere weaken orientation. Forms need helpful labels, constraints, validation, success, and recoverable error copy.

Replace abstract promises with observable specifics. Avoid empty "empower", "revolutionize", "one-stop solution", and their Chinese equivalents unless they communicate a real scoped claim. A brand voice can be expressive while the actions stay clear.

Establish claim→explanation→action→supporting detail, then check reading length. Chinese headings should break at meaningful phrases. Test shortest and longest plausible titles, English compounds, mixed scripts, numbers, filenames, and user-entered text. Do not reduce important type size to conceal overflow. Keep decorative labels only when they add information; repeated labels are not hierarchy.

If real content is missing, use an obvious placeholder or ask about a critical business gap. Sample copy is acceptable for a clearly marked mechanism demonstration, never as an implied real customer claim.
